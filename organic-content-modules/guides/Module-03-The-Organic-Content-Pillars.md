## Slide 10: The Organic Content Pillars

**Say:**
- Four pillars. Every post you make fits one of them, so you never start from a blank page again.
- Connection, at the top. Do they like you? Show your character, your day and your team, or they feel a disconnect and never meet the person behind the firm.
- Value, on the right. This is where most lawyers live, and it is great, just not on its own. Pain points, challenges, and simplifying concepts. Be helpful, not lecturing. One slice of the cake per video.
- Proof, on the left. They are thinking: is this person legit, who have they actually helped? Testimonials, case studies, and the screenshots of thank-you messages you already get. Always with permission, never identifying anyone.
- Invitation, at the bottom. Invite them to start: a free resource, an event, a workshop. Do not sell. Give publicly, sell privately, and let your ads do the selling.

## Slide 11: Pillar 1: Connection

**Say:**
- Connection. The question in their head: Do I like you? Do you get what I'm going through?
- The trap: Disconnect: only tips and results, so they never meet the person behind the firm.
- Character: Your opinions, your beliefs, what you go against the grain on. For example: "Unpopular opinion: most separations should never go near a courtroom."
- Day: Document what you already do: court, meetings, the drive, the 7am coffee. For example: "What a Tuesday actually looks like for a family lawyer."
- Team: Put faces to the firm. Collab posts with the people clients will deal with. For example: "This is [name]. If you've ever [problem], she's who you want."

## Slide 12: Pillar 2: Value

**Say:**
- Value. The question in their head: Is this helpful? Do I understand my situation better now?
- The trap: Lecturing: "you should do this", too detailed, too much in one video.
- Pain points: The thing keeping your clients up at night, answered honestly. For example: "'Will I lose the house?' Here's the honest answer."
- Challenges / obstacles: Call out the exact situation they're stuck in, then show the way through. For example: "If you've just separated and don't know what to do first, watch this."
- Simplifying concepts: Take a term or process people get stuck on and say it in plain English. For example: "What a binding financial agreement actually is, in 30 seconds."

## Slide 13: Pillar 3: Proof

**Say:**
- Proof. The question in their head: Are you legit? Who have you actually helped?
- The trap: Disbelief: great talker, but no sign of real clients or results.
- Testimonial: A client on camera in their own words, with written permission. For example: "Don't take it from me. Here's what [client] said."
- Case study: Start with the outcome, then walk through how you got there. De-identified. For example: "A client came to us after [obstacle]. [X] months later, [outcome]."
- Screenshots: The thank-you emails and texts you already get. Names blurred, with permission. For example: "This message made our whole week."

## Slide 14: Pillar 4: Invitation

**Say:**
- Invitation. The question in their head: How do I get started with you?
- The trap: Selling: a pitch in every post. Give publicly, sell privately. Let your ads do the selling.
- Resource: A free checklist, guide or short training they get by commenting a keyword. For example: "I made a checklist of everything to bring to your first meeting. Comment CHECKLIST."
- Events: Meet people in person: a free Q&A night, a coffee meetup, a local talk. For example: "We're running a free Q&A night in [suburb]. Comment SEAT for details."
- Workshops: A low-cost, deeper session for people who've been following for a while. For example: "Want to go deeper on this? I'm running a 2-hour workshop on [date]."

## Slide 16: Make More Content, Faster

**Say:**
- Four ways to make more content without more ideas.
- Collab posts: your team members have different specialties. If you have finance in-house, get your accountant to answer the money questions clients ask you.
- Run a series: one topic, over and over. Talk about the news: you bring some of the most nuanced views in society. And come on a podcast with us.

## Slide 17: The Outlier Method

**Say:**
- Viral is not luck. Find a video from a small or mid-sized account that got five times their normal views. That is an outlier.
- Ignore the topic. Look at the structure: how the hook works, how the points are ordered, how it ends.
- Then pour your expertise into that same structure. You can paste the transcript into Claude and ask it to turn it into a fill-in-the-blank template.
- Copy the shape, never the words.
